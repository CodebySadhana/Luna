# YouTube Shorts

## Posture
Same browsing posture as TikTok, but on an app where the viewer may also watch
long-form. That second fact is the whole strategic point.

## Constraints that shape content
- Vertical, under 3 minutes, sound-on.
- Lives on a channel that may also host long-form — a Short can send someone
  to a real video.
- Comments and subscriptions carry across to the main channel.

## What makes it different from TikTok
**Shorts are a top-of-funnel for a channel, not a destination.** On TikTok the
piece is the product. Here, the piece can be a door.

This changes the ending. A TikTok ends by landing the idea. A Short can end by
landing the idea *and* pointing at the deeper version.

## Hooks
Same brutal first second as TikTok. Same rules apply — nothing new to learn.

## Structure that works
Identical to TikTok, with one addition: if a long-form video exists on the
same subject, the Short should be a genuine excerpt or a genuine teaser, not
a trailer. Trailers don't convert; a complete small idea that implies a larger
one does.

## For a solo operator
The cheapest way to give a long-form channel a chance at cold reach. If you
make long videos and no Shorts, you're relying entirely on search.

Repurposing rule: don't clip a long video at random. Find the 30 seconds that
stands alone as a complete thought, and re-cut it with its own hook.

## Don't
- Upload a TikTok with a visible watermark
- End with "full video on my channel" and nothing else — say what's in it
- Assume TikTok performance transfers. Different audience, often different result.
