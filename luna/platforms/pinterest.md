# Pinterest

## Posture
Planning, not browsing. Someone is looking for something to *do* or *buy*,
often weeks or months before they do it. Closer to a search engine than a
social network — and it should be treated as one.

## Constraints that shape content
- Vertical images, 2:3, are the standard unit.
- Text on the image is read as part of the result — titles matter visually.
- Links work, and they work well. Rare among social platforms.
- Content surfaces for months or years. The longest tail in social, by far.

## What the format rewards
**Searchable, specific, visually legible.** The pin is a search result that
happens to be a picture.

Titles and descriptions do real work here, because real search is happening.

## For a solo operator
The most-ignored platform for anyone whose content has a visual or
instructional component — food, design, home, fitness, craft, planning,
templates, printables.

**Traffic is the point.** If you have anything to link to — a newsletter, a
product, a guide — this is one of the only social surfaces where a link is a
first-class citizen.

The effort profile is unusual: work published today may do nothing for six
weeks, then compound quietly for a year. Judge it on a quarter, not a week.

## Structure that works
- One clear subject per pin, legible at small size
- Text overlay stating the specific outcome
- Title and description written for search, in plain language
- Several pin variants pointing at the same destination

## Don't
- Treat it like Instagram. Different posture, different content, different metric.
- Judge performance in the first two weeks
- Pin without a destination — the link is the whole advantage
