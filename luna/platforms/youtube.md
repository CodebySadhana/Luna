# YouTube (long-form)

## Posture
Two different viewers, and they need different content:
- **Search** — has a specific question, high intent, will tolerate depth.
- **Browse** — bored on a TV or a laptop, picking from a wall of thumbnails.

Know which one a piece is for before making it. A video that serves both
usually serves neither.

## Constraints that shape content
- Title and thumbnail are the entire pitch. They are chosen before anything
  is watched.
- Horizontal 16:9. Often on a large screen.
- Descriptions support links, chapters, and real text.
- The single longest content half-life in social. A good video keeps working
  for years — the only platform where that is reliably true.

## What the format rewards
**Watch time and the click-then-stay combination.** A thumbnail that
over-promises gets the click and loses everything after it.

## Packaging is 80% of the work
Title and thumbnail deserve more iteration than the edit. Write the title
first — if you can't write a compelling one, the idea isn't there yet.

Thumbnail: one subject, legible at postage-stamp size, no more than three or
four words of text.

## Structure that works
```
0–15s   restate the promise from the title and prove you'll deliver
15–45s  the stakes — why this matters, briefly
body    deliver in order, one section per idea, chapters marked
end     the actual conclusion, not a summary of what they just watched
```

Do not save the answer for the end. Deliver value early and continuously —
withholding is a Shorts tactic and it fails at length.

## For a solo operator
The highest cost per piece and the highest long-term return. One genuinely
good search-intent video can outperform a year of feed posts, because it keeps
being found.

If you make one long-form thing, make it the definitive answer to a question
your audience actually types.

## Don't
- Titles that are clever instead of clear — search doesn't reward wordplay
- Padding to hit an arbitrary length
- A thumbnail that promises something the video doesn't contain
