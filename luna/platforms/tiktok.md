# TikTok

## Posture
Bored and browsing, with a very low threshold for leaving. The viewer has no
relationship with you and owes you nothing. Almost all reach is cold.

## Constraints that shape content
- Vertical 9:16, sound-on by default (the one major platform where it is).
- No links in captions for most accounts.
- The exit is one thumb-flick away, always.
- Comments are unusually prominent and drive a second wave of watching.

## What the format rewards
**Watch-through and re-watch.** This is the platform where a piece finishing
strong actually matters, because the loop back to the start is free.

**Cold reach is the highest of any platform**, which makes TikTok the best
place to *test an idea*. If a concept can't hold strangers here, it probably
isn't the audience's fault.

## Hooks
The most demanding first second in social. Not "in this video I'll show you" —
start mid-action, mid-argument, or mid-confession.

Say the interesting thing first. There is no runway.

## Structure that works
```
0–1s    the claim, the contradiction, or the visual anomaly
1–3s    one concrete detail that proves this is going somewhere
3–10s   the turn — not what they expected
10s+    one new beat at a time, no recaps
end     land it. A strong last line drives the re-watch and the comment.
```

Under 30 seconds unless the idea genuinely earns more. Length is not
ambition, it's a bill the viewer pays.

## Comments are the second engine
A deliberate open question, or one detail left unexplained, produces a comment
section that keeps the piece alive. Answer comments with new videos — the
cheapest content you will ever make and the most relevant.

## For a solo operator
The fastest place to find out whether an idea is any good, at the lowest
production cost. Low polish is not a penalty here — it can be an advantage.

## Don't
- Recycle a horizontal video with bars
- Slow intros, logo animations, "hey guys welcome back"
- Chase a sound you don't understand — see Principle 02 of the rules
