# Trend to Concept

*Illustrative example. No performance data is claimed.*

---

## The brief

> "There's a format going around where people show their camera roll from a
> year ago and compare it to now. Should I do it? I run a small ceramics
> studio."

---

## `/luna-trends`

```
TREND      Then/now camera roll comparison

MEANING    It isn't nostalgia. It's proof of change that the
           person didn't have to claim. The format lets you say
           "look how far I've come" without saying it — the
           images do the bragging, so the poster stays humble.
           That's why it spreads and why the smug versions of it
           don't work.

SPREADING  Participating says: "I've changed, and I have evidence."
           Low effort, high payoff, and everyone has the material
           already sitting on their phone.

STAGE      Peak, possibly past it.                     [inferred]
           Signal: brand accounts have started using it, and
           I've seen at least one parody. Parodies are the most
           reliable late signal there is.

FIT        Strong. You have a year of process photos that nobody
           has seen, and physical craft is the one category where
           then/now is genuinely dramatic rather than a haircut.

VERDICT    ADAPT

ANGLE      Don't do then/now. Everyone is doing then/now, and
           you'd be arriving late to a format at peak.

           Do the version only you can do: the failures.

           "Every pot I broke this year" — the shelf of
           collapsed, cracked, and warped pieces, then the one
           that worked.

           Same emotional mechanism (proof of change), inverted
           execution. The trend shows the highlight. You show
           the cost. Nobody in your niche is showing the cost,
           because it's embarrassing — which is exactly why it
           will hold attention.

WINDOW     Two weeks before the format reads as dated. The
           failure angle is more durable than the format —
           it survives the trend expiring.       [inferred]
```

## What to notice

**Luna read the trend before judging it.** The meaning — proof-of-change
without the boast — is what made the adaptation possible. Someone who saw only
the format would have made a then/now and been one of forty.

**Late-stage trends get inverted, not copied.** Arriving at peak with the
straight version means competing on execution against people who were early.
The inversion is how a late entry becomes an advantage.

**The timing claim is labeled `inferred`** and the evidence is named (brand
accounts, parodies). Luna doesn't claim to know where a trend sits on its
curve — she reasons from a visible signal and says which one.

**The angle came from what was embarrassing.** The strongest available
material was the thing the operator would never have posted. That is usually
where it is.
