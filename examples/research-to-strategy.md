# Research to Strategy

Comments → tension → positioning → pillars. The full front half of the loop.

*Illustrative example. No performance data is claimed.*

---

## The brief

> "I'm a bookkeeper. I want to grow on LinkedIn but I don't know what to post
> that isn't boring."

---

## `/luna-observe`

```
AUDIENCE
  Situation:   Small business owners, 1-10 staff, who do their own
               books badly and know it. Not accountants. Not
               finance teams. People who have a shoebox.

  Their words:  [observed, from thread replies and review sections]
    "I know I'm probably doing this wrong but it works"
    "My accountant just sighs at me once a year"
    "I don't want to look stupid asking"
    "I'll deal with it in January"

  Stated want:  cleaner books
  Real want:    to stop feeling like a fraud about money   [inferred]

TENSION
  This is a shame problem, not a knowledge problem. The
  information is free and everywhere. They aren't googling
  because googling means admitting they don't know — and every
  piece of bookkeeping content they meet is written in a tone
  that confirms they should have known already.

  Nobody is talking to them without condescension.

LANDSCAPE
  Everyone says:  "5 bookkeeping mistakes you're making"
                  (accusatory, from an expert, downward)
  Nobody says:    "here is the mistake I see constantly and it is
                  completely reasonable that you made it"
  The gap:        the absence of judgment

CONFIDENCE
  observed:  the four quoted phrases
  inferred:  the shame framing
  guess:     that the gap is unoccupied — verify by reading the
             top 20 accounts in this niche before committing
```

---

## `/luna-strategy`

```
POSITION    The bookkeeper who doesn't make you feel stupid.
NOT FOR     Other bookkeepers. Finance teams. Anyone who wants
            technical depth — that audience is bigger and already
            served, and serving both is what makes an account
            unreadable.
POV         Most small business owners aren't bad with money.
            They were handed a system built for accountants and
            told it was for them.
ADVANTAGE   Candor. A firm cannot say "this rule is stupid and
            here's how everyone gets around it legally." You can.
AUDIENCE    The shoebox owner who plans to deal with it in January

PILLARS
  01  "The mistake I see every week"
      Always framed as reasonable. Never as a failing.
  02  "What your accountant is too polite to tell you"
      Insider register. High share value.
  03  "Ten minutes, once a month"
      The smallest version of doing it right. Anti-overwhelm.

PRIMARY     LinkedIn — the audience is there in a work frame, and
            text carries this better than video would
SECONDARY   Email — the only audience a platform can't take from
            you, and this content is reference-shaped
CADENCE     2 posts a week. You have a business to run. Four would
            be better and you would stop in March.

TRADE-OFF   You give up the professional-peer audience entirely.
            Other bookkeepers will find your content simplistic.
            Some will say so. That's the position working, not
            failing.

FIRST MOVE  Post pillar 01 this week. The exact phrase to open
            with is already in your research:
            "I know I'm probably doing this wrong but it works."
            That's the post. Start there.
```

---

## What to notice

**The strategy came out of the comments, not out of Luna.** Every element
traces to something the audience actually said. The positioning is
"non-judgmental" because four separate quotes were about feeling stupid.

**One sentence separates this from every competitor.** Not a channel tactic —
a tone that the incumbents structurally can't adopt, because a firm has to
protect its authority.

**The trade-off is explicit and uncomfortable.** Giving up peers means peers
will criticize the content. Saying this in advance is what makes the strategy
survivable.

**The confidence labels stayed on.** "The gap is unoccupied" is marked as a
guess with a specific way to verify it. That is the one claim that could sink
the whole strategy, and it is flagged as the thing to check first.

**The cadence is deliberately low.** Two posts a week from a working
bookkeeper beats four for six weeks and then nothing.
