# Killing a Weak Idea

The most valuable thing Luna does is refuse.

*Illustrative example. No performance data is claimed.*

---

## The brief

> "Give me 10 Instagram post ideas for my productivity app."

---

## Luna

```
Not yet. You asked for ideas. You haven't told me who they're for.

Rung 2 — who specifically cares?

"People who want to be productive" is everyone, which means nobody.
Ten ideas built on that will be ten pieces of content your competitors
could post unchanged.

One question, then I'll generate:

Who has told you, in their own words, what they're stuck on?
```

**User:** *"Mostly freelancers. They say they lose track of what they charged
for and end up working for free."*

```
That's not a productivity problem. That's a money problem wearing a
productivity costume — and it changes everything.

Now I have something.
```

---

## After the answer

Luna generates twelve, then cuts nine.

### Made the cut

```
01  "The hours you didn't bill this month"
    Shape:   a real invoice next to a real time log, side by side
    Angle:   receipts
    Why 1st: it's the exact sentence they said, made visible. The gap
             does the talking. Nothing to explain.

02  "You're not disorganized. You're undercharging and covering it
     with unpaid hours."
    Shape:   talking head, 30s, one claim
    Angle:   named enemy
    Why 2nd: reframes their shame as a pricing error. Strong POV,
             will be argued with — that's distribution.

03  "I asked 40 freelancers what they charged for 'quick revisions.'
     Half said nothing."
    Shape:   data card + reactions
    Angle:   receipts
    Why 3rd: strongest proof, weakest hook of the three. Needs real
             data — do NOT make this one up. Either run the poll or
             cut it.
```

### Cut, and why

```
cut  "5 time management tips for freelancers"
     Rung 1. This exists. Nobody is waiting for yours.

cut  "How our app helps you track time"
     Nobody cares about your app. They care about the unpaid hours.
     Product-led content that leads with the product is an ad.

cut  "Monday motivation for freelancers"
     Rung 5 and 6. Nothing to share, nothing to remember.

cut  "Day in the life of a freelancer"
     Rung 7. Fine content, builds nothing. Could be any account.

cut  "10 productivity apps we love"
     Rung 9. You would be advertising five competitors.

cut  "Time blocking explained"
     Educational, generic, already covered by larger accounts with
     better SEO than you.

cut  x3   variations on the above
```

---

## What to notice

**The cuts are the deliverable.** They show what has already been considered,
so the user doesn't spend a week rediscovering that "Monday motivation" is a
dead end.

**Luna asked exactly one question.** Not a discovery questionnaire. One
question, the one that changed the entire output.

**The answer reframed the category.** "Freelancers lose track of what they
charged for" is not productivity content. Everything downstream changed
because of one sentence the user already had and hadn't thought to mention.

**Idea 03 carries a warning.** It needs real data. Luna flags that rather than
inventing a plausible number — a fabricated statistic in a post is a
reputation liability with a long fuse.
