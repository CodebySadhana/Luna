# Task 02 — Specificity

**Status: not yet run.**

## What this measures

Whether output uses concrete nouns or abstractions. Luna's Principle 02 says
generic content is invisible; this checks whether the skill actually changes
the words.

## Method

Eight hook-writing prompts, each with a specific detail already present in the
brief. The detail is available — the question is whether the arm uses it.

```
S1  Brief: "I spent $2,400 on home gym equipment and used it 11 times."
S2  Brief: "43 of the 50 setups I audited had never opened settings."
S3  Brief: "I broke 40 pots this year learning to centre clay."
S4  Brief: "My best email subject line was 4 words long."
S5  Brief: "I tripled posting for 60 days. Followers went up 2%."
S6  Brief: "A client paid me for 6 hours. I worked 19."
S7  Brief: "Three of my students failed the same question."
S8  Brief: "I rewrote the landing page 14 times before it converted."
```

## Scoring

Two countable measures per output:

**1 — Detail retention.** Does the hook use the specific number or detail from
the brief? Binary, 0 or 1.

**2 — Abstraction count.** Occurrences of a fixed vague-word list, per 100
words:

```
a lot, many, several, some, various, significant, effective,
powerful, amazing, incredible, game-changing, leverage, optimize,
best practices, key takeaways, actionable insights, unlock,
elevate, transform, boost, supercharge
```

The list is fixed in advance and applied identically to every arm.

```
                  detail kept      abstractions / 100 words
A  no skill          _ / 8                   _
B  generic prompt    _ / 8                   _
C  Luna              _ / 8                   _
```

## Why this is objective

Both measures are counts against a list fixed before the run. No judgement.

## Known limitation

Low abstraction count does not prove a hook is good — a terse bad hook scores
well. This measures one property Luna claims, not overall quality. Do not
present it as a quality score.
