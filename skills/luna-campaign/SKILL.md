# Luna Campaign

A campaign is content with a **spine**: one goal, one story, an ending.

If the pieces would work equally well in any order, it isn't a campaign. It's
a posting schedule.

## The only case for all ten minds

Miranda (goal, what's given up) → Harvey (sequence, advantage) → Sherlock
(what the audience already believes) → Midge (the through-line) → Blair (the
lines) → David (what actually gets made) → Olivia (how it travels) → Elle
(what the audience does) → Spencer (what tells you it worked) → Tony (what
gets templated).

Run in that order. The output is one plan, not ten sections.

## The spine

Every campaign answers these before any content is planned:

1. **One goal, measurable.** Sales, signups, waitlist, applications. "Awareness"
   is not a goal — it is what you say when you don't have one.
2. **One story.** What changes for the audience, in one sentence.
3. **The objection.** The real reason they won't act. The campaign exists to
   dismantle it. If you can't name it, go back to `/luna-observe`.
4. **The date.** A campaign with no ending is a content strategy.

## The arc

```
BEFORE     Establish the problem. Do not mention the thing yet.
           Longest phase. Most operators skip it and then wonder
           why the launch landed on people with no context.

TENSION    Name the objection out loud, before they do.
           Credibility comes from saying the hard part first.

LAUNCH     The offer, stated plainly, once. Then again in a
           different form for people who missed it.

AFTER      Proof, results, what happened. This is also the
           beginning of the next campaign.
```

Most solo launches fail in BEFORE. They compress weeks of context-building
into three days of announcements to an audience that was never prepared.

## The solo-operator constraint

**Plan for the worst week, not the best one.** A campaign requiring daily
output for three weeks will be abandoned in week two, and an abandoned launch
is worse than a smaller one — it teaches the audience that your announcements
don't mean anything.

Rule: **make the BEFORE phase in advance.** If it isn't made before LAUNCH
starts, it doesn't exist.

## Output

```
GOAL        [measurable, with a number]
STORY       [one sentence]
OBJECTION   [the real reason they won't]
DATES       [start → launch → end]

BEFORE   (dates)   [3–5 pieces, each with its job]
TENSION  (dates)   [1–2 pieces, the objection handled]
LAUNCH   (dates)   [the offer + the re-statement]
AFTER    (dates)   [proof, results]

CHANNELS    primary / secondary, and what each does
MAKE AHEAD  [everything that must exist before day one]
SUCCESS     [the number that means it worked]
FAILURE     [the number that means stop and rethink]
```

Both SUCCESS and FAILURE, written before launch. A campaign with only a
success condition can never be honestly evaluated.

## Boundaries

No fabricated projections. If asked "how many sales will this get," the answer
is that Luna doesn't know, and here's what to measure to find out after week
one.
