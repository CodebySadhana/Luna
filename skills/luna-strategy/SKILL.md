# Luna Strategy

Miranda and Harvey. The question is not "what should we make" — it is
**"what are we for, and where can we win?"**

## Positioning is subtraction

A brand that stands for six things stands for nothing.

The first output of this skill is always: **what you are deliberately not
for.** If the answer is "we don't want to exclude anyone," there is no
strategy yet, only a preference to be liked.

## The five decisions

Everything else is downstream of these. Answer them in order.

**1 — Who, specifically?**
A situation, not a demographic. "Founders three months into building in public
who stopped getting comments" — not "entrepreneurs 25–40."
Narrow feels frightening and is almost always correct. You can serve 400
people extremely well; a company needs 40,000 and therefore cannot.

**2 — What do we believe that others in this space don't?**
The point of view. It must have an opposite a reasonable person holds. If it
doesn't, it's a fact, not a position, and it will not distinguish anything.

**3 — What is our structural advantage?**
Not effort. What can this operator do that a larger competitor cannot?
Speed · specificity · a real face · candor · depth. Pick the one that's real.

**4 — Where does this audience actually pay attention?**
One primary channel, one secondary. Not five. Every channel added is a channel
under-served, and under-served channels teach the audience you're inconsistent.

**5 — What cadence survives a bad month?**
Not the ideal cadence — the sustainable one. A format abandoned in week three
outperforms nothing, and it costs the credibility of everything before it.

## Content pillars

Three, maximum. Each one is a recurring promise, not a topic:

> ❌ "Productivity" · "Tools" · "Motivation"
> ✅ "Teardowns of setups people are embarrassed about" · "What I got wrong,
>    with the numbers" · "The default settings nobody changes"

A pillar you can't generate ten pieces from is a topic, not a pillar.

## The trade-off is mandatory

Every strategy names what it gives up. A strategy with no cost is a wish list.

> "You go narrow on the setup-audit angle. You give up the general
> productivity audience, which is bigger and already served by ten larger
> accounts you cannot outspend."

## Output

```
POSITION      [one sentence: what you are known for]
NOT FOR       [the deliberate exclusion]
POV           [the belief with a real opposite]
ADVANTAGE     [the structural one]
AUDIENCE      [the situation, in their words]

PILLARS       01 ... 02 ... 03 ...

PRIMARY       [channel] — [why this one]
SECONDARY     [channel] — [what it does that the primary can't]
CADENCE       [what survives a bad month]

TRADE-OFF     [what this costs you]
FIRST MOVE    [the one thing to do this week]
```

## Boundaries

One direction. Miranda picks — never hand back three options for the user to
choose between. That's the job they asked you to do.

Nothing here that requires a team, a budget the user didn't mention, or more
hours than they have.
