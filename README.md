## The internet doesn't need more content.

It has more content than anyone will ever read.

Every AI marketing tool solves the wrong problem. They make posting faster.
Posting was never the bottleneck — a person with a phone can publish forty
times a day. The bottleneck is that almost none of it deserves to be seen.

So the tools produce more of what nobody was waiting for, faster.

**Luna is built on the opposite assumption:** attention is earned before
content is created, and the highest-value thing a marketing tool can do is
tell you not to make something.

## The Flex

One brief. Two versions. This is the whole product.

> **The brief:** *"I want to post something about productivity for students."*

Luna's first response to that brief was not a better headline. It was:

```
Kill it.

Rung 1 — does anyone actually care?
No. This has been posted ten thousand times.

Rung 2 — who specifically cares?
"Students" is not an audience. It's a census category.
```

Then she went and looked at what students actually say:

> *"I have the perfect system and I'm still behind."*
> *"My notes are beautiful. I don't remember any of it."*

**That's the tension.** The people asking for productivity advice are already
the ones taking it. Their systems became the work.

The hook wasn't the work. **The observation was.**

## She doesn't make content. She studies attention.

She watches what people stop for. What they share. What they ignore. What
suddenly starts appearing everywhere.

Then she asks one question:

> ## **"Why would anyone care?"**

If she can't answer it, she doesn't make the post.

If she can, she turns the insight into an idea, the idea into a hook, and the
hook into something worth stopping for.

Luna doesn't believe in posting more. She believes in **making fewer things
matter more.**

## How she thinks

```
        OBSERVE  →  UNDERSTAND  →  STRATEGIZE
           ↑                             ↓
        LEARN    ←     TEST     ←     CREATE
```

Nothing enters CREATE that hasn't survived OBSERVE. Nothing leaves TEST
without updating memory. **The loop is the product.**

## The Luna Ladder

Ponytail asks *can this be built with less code?* Luna asks
**does this deserve anyone's attention?**

Fail a rung, stop there. The rung you failed is the finding.

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **01** | Does anyone actually care? | fail → **KILL** |
| **02** | Who specifically cares? | fail → go research |
| **03** | Why would they stop? | fail → the hook is dead, not the idea |
| **04** | Why would they keep watching? | |
| **05** | Why would they share it? | |
| **06** | Why would they remember it? | |
| **07** | Does it strengthen the brand? | |
| **08** | Can the idea be sharper? | *always run this one* |
| **09** | Can the execution be more distinctive? | |
| **10** | **TEST** | publish with a written expectation |
| **11** | **LEARN** | record the delta, not the number |
| **12** | **REPEAT** | |

> ### **Don't create content. Create reasons for people to care.**

Every run ends on a verdict: **MAKE IT** · **REWORK IT** · **KILL IT**.
Never "here are some thoughts."

## She remembers

Luna without memory is a very opinionated stranger.

```
luna/memory/
├── brand.md         who you are, what you're not
├── audience.md      their exact words
├── wins.md          what worked — and the mechanism
├── losses.md        what failed. The most valuable file here.
├── patterns.md      what held up across 3+ instances
└── experiments.md   what's being tested right now
```

Five record types: **WIN · LOSS · PATTERN · HYPOTHESIS · EXPERIMENT**

Plain markdown, in your repo, that you can read and correct. She records the
*mechanism*, never just the outcome — "it worked" is not reusable. She shows
you the diff before writing. And she never writes a number you didn't give
her.

## Architecture

**Luna has no runtime.** No engine, no pipeline, no database. Every file is an
instruction, an adapter generated from one, or a test guarding one.

```
skills/               21 skills — the source of truth
luna/soul/            ladder, principles, voice, scoring, orchestration
luna/minds/           the ten minds
luna/platforms/       8 platforms, reasoned from constraints
luna/memory/          the learning loop
commands/  .opencode/  .cursor/  .agents/     ← all generated
```

Core intelligence lives once. Every host adapter is generated by
`scripts/sync_adapters.py` from the skills, and CI fails if one drifts — so no
agent can quietly run last month's rules.

→ [Architecture](docs/architecture.md)

## Benchmarks

**Not yet measured.** No performance figure appears anywhere in this
repository.

Ponytail can publish hard numbers because "lines of code" is countable from a
single completion. Luna's claim — *this idea deserved attention* — isn't
settled that way: the ground truth is downstream and confounded, the correct
output is often "don't make this" (which looks like failure in any volume
metric), and an LLM judging LLM marketing copy reliably prefers the fluent
generic text that Luna exists to prevent.

So instead of a fabricated scoreboard, `benchmarks/` specifies four things
that *can* be measured objectively — refusal rate, specificity, **fabrication
resistance**, and words-per-decision — with the arms, prompts, and scoring
fixed in advance.

They have not been run. When they are, the results go here with the model,
date, temperature, run count, and the failures.

→ [Benchmark methodology](benchmarks/)

## What she refuses

Guaranteed virality. Invented metrics, trends, or benchmarks. Claimed
knowledge of any ranking algorithm. Engagement bait, pods, fake comments, fake
social proof. Copying a competitor's content. Ten ideas with no ranking.
Content made because it is Tuesday.

**If you gave her no data, she has no data.** She'll say so.

## Who she's for

Personal brands, founders, and small businesses growing an audience. One
person, sometimes two.

If advice needs a department, a budget you didn't mention, or a six-week
sprint, Luna considers it the wrong advice — and rewrites it for someone with
a phone and four hours a week.
